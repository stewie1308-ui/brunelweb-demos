New patients

A long appointment,
and no lecture.

Forty-five minutes for a first visit. We look, we scan, and you tell us what has actually been bothering you. Nothing gets done to you on the day unless you ask for it.

If it has been years

That is most of our new patients. It is not a conversation you need to brace for, and nobody here is going to make you feel worse about it than you already do.

The first appointment is an assessment. We will tell you what we find and what the options are, in writing, and then you go home and think about it.

If you are frightened

Say so when you book, and it goes on your notes so you do not have to explain it again to three different people.

Practical things that help, and which we do routinely: longer appointments so nothing is rushed, a stop signal agreed before we start, and treatment broken into shorter visits. Sedation is available for people who need it, and it is not a failure to ask.

  • Tell us when booking, not on the day
  • An agreed signal to stop, at any point
  • Nothing done at the first visit unless you ask
  • Sedation available for longer treatment

Practical things

We are a wholly private practice and do not take NHS patients. We say so up front because it is the first thing worth knowing, and if what you need would be better done on the NHS we will tell you that too.

If you are moving from another practice, ask them for your records and X-rays. It saves repeating radiographs and it is your right to have them.

Before your first visit

What should I bring?

A list of any medication you take, and details of your previous dentist if you have one. Nothing else. If you have had X-rays elsewhere in the last year, ask them to send them over so we do not repeat them unnecessarily.

Will you tell me off?

No. It achieves nothing and it is the reason a lot of people stopped going in the first place. We are interested in what you want to fix and what is realistic, not in how long it has been.

Can I just have a clean?

Yes. The hygienist is bookable without seeing a dentist first, at £75 for thirty minutes. If we spot something during it we will mention it, and that is as far as it goes.
