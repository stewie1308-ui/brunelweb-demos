Planting

Chalk-tolerant,
and left to fill.

Thin alkaline soil rules out half the catalogue. We plant what actually thrives on the Downs, and space it to close up rather than look finished on day one.

What works here

Steppe and Mediterranean planting, essentially. Things that evolved on thin dry alkaline ground and resent being fed or watered once established.

  • Grasses: stipa, molinia, calamagrostis, deschampsia
  • Perennials: verbena bonariensis, eryngium, achillea, nepeta, salvia, scabious
  • Shrubs: cistus, phlomis, rosemary, lavender
  • Not: rhododendron, camellia, pieris, blueberry, most acid-lovers

Spacing, and the awkward first year

We plant at the spacing the plants will want in year three, which means it looks sparse in year one. That is deliberate. Planting for instant effect means lifting half of it out in two years, or watching it strangle itself.

If a garden has to read well immediately for an event or a sale, say so and we will plant denser and tell you what will need removing later.

Getting it established

Everything goes in between October and March where we can, so the roots get down before the ground dries. Autumn planting on chalk is worth roughly a year of growth over spring.

Watering for the first summer is the client’s job and it is the one thing that decides whether the planting takes. We leave a schedule and we are blunt about it.

Planting plans on their own are fine too.

Plenty of people have the hard landscaping already and want the borders sorted. That is a smaller job and we are happy to do it.
