Levels first.

We survey the fall of the site before anything is drawn, because on a sloping chalk garden the levels decide everything that follows.

The survey

We measure levels across the whole plot, not just where the work is going. A terrace that works on its own can still dump water at a wall forty feet away, and on chalk the water goes somewhere fast.

We also dig two or three inspection holes. Depth of topsoil over chalk is the single number that decides what will grow, and it is rarely the same across one garden.

What you get

A scaled layout plan, a levels and setting-out drawing, a planting plan with quantities, and a written specification any competent contractor could price.

  • Scaled layout and levels drawings
  • Planting plan with species and spacings
  • Written specification and schedule of works
  • The drawings are yours, whoever builds it

Designing for chalk

Thin alkaline soil over free-draining rock means a garden that bakes by July and never waterlogs. That rules out acid-lovers entirely and makes irrigation a design decision rather than an afterthought.

It also means you can plant things here that rot anywhere else. The right response is a drier, looser, more Mediterranean garden, not a lawn fighting for its life.
