The workshop

A banker, a mallet,
and no saw marks.

Everything is set out full size and finished with a chisel. It costs more in hours, and it is the whole reason the work looks like it belongs.

Setting out

Every piece is drawn full size on a board before a chisel touches stone. Zinc templates are cut from that drawing, and the templates are what get marked onto the block.

It is the slowest part of the job and it is where mistakes are cheap. A wrong line on a board costs a morning. A wrong line on the block costs the block.

Worked, not sawn

A saw leaves a flat, dead face that reads as machine-made from across a room. A chisel leaves tooling that catches the light differently along its length.

On restoration this matters twice over, because a sawn face beside a hand-worked Georgian one announces itself immediately.

Where we work

The workshop is in Chippenham and we fit across Wiltshire and the Cotswolds. Regular work in Marlborough, Cirencester, Royal Wootton Bassett, Malmesbury, Devizes and Swindon.

Further for the right job, though a long haul on a single fireplace lintel rarely makes sense for anybody.
